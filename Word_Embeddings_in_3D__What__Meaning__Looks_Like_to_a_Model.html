Word Embeddings in 3D: What "Meaning" Looks Like to a Model

A language model represents every word or phrase as a long list of numbers, a vector, computed by a real embedding model. Words used in similar contexts end up with similar vectors. Below, real embeddings for 24 words have been computed in your browser and compressed down to 3 dimensions so you can see, and rotate, that structure directly.

Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom. Each axis (PC1, PC2, PC3) is a mathematical direction found by Principal Component Analysis; it has no meaning on its own. What matters is distance: words the model treats as similar end up close together in this space, regardless of the category colors, which were added afterward only so you can see the clusters.

2. The question this raises

The model was never told "dog" and "cat" are both animals, or that "happy" and "joyful" mean similar things. It only ever saw these words used in huge amounts of real text. Why would words with related meanings end up close together in this space purely from that? And what happens to a word that's ambiguous, like "bank" (a river bank vs. a financial bank)?
